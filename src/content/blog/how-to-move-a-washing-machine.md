Knowing how to move a washing machine properly saves you from a common moving-day problem: a machine that shakes, bangs or leaks the first time it's used in its new home. The short version is to drain it, lock the drum with the transit bolts, keep it upright, and set it up level with the bolts removed at the other end.

Below are the steps for front loaders and top loaders, why the transit bolts matter so much, how to move the machine without hurting yourself or the floors, and what to check once it's connected again.

## Why the transit bolts matter

A front loader's drum doesn't sit on a fixed base. It hangs inside the cabinet on springs and shock absorbers, so it can move freely during the spin. That's good for washing, but on the back of a bakkie or in a moving truck, an unlocked drum swings and bangs with every bump.

Transit bolts (also called shipping bolts or transport bolts) are long bolts that screw in through the back of the machine to hold the drum still. AEG explains that they stop the drum moving and prevent damage to the shock absorbers and suspension, the hoses and cables, the motor and the heavy counterweights that steady the drum. Samsung's South African support site gives the same advice: refit the shipping bolts before you move the machine, and take them out again before you use it at your new home.

They matter just as much at the other end. Running a machine with the bolts still fitted locks the suspension solid, and AEG warns that the machine will vibrate heavily, bang loudly and can be permanently damaged.

## Before moving day

- **Find the transit bolts.** If they were kept, they're often in a bag taped inside a cupboard or in the original packaging. Most front loaders have three or four.
- **Find the right spanner.** The size differs between brands: Bosch specifies a 17 mm spanner for its transport bolts, AEG a 10 mm one, and some brands, including Samsung, supply a spanner with the machine.
- **Lost the bolts?** Order a replacement set using the model number on the machine's rating sticker. AEG recommends getting the correct kit for your model before you transport the machine.
- **Download the manual** if you don't have it. It shows where the bolts go and how to drain the machine.
- **Check the new home.** Make sure there's a cold water tap, a drain point and a proper socket where the machine will stand. If there isn't, a plumber or electrician needs to add them before the move.

## Step by step: getting the machine ready

### 1. Do a last wash a day or two before

Wash, then wipe the door seal dry and leave the door and drawer open so the inside dries, with the laundry door closed if small children or pets are around. Beko suggests cleaning the machine before a move so mould doesn't grow inside it during the trip, and our guide to [cleaning a washing machine](/blog/how-to-clean-a-washing-machine/) covers each part. A dry machine is lighter and won't drip in the truck.

### 2. Switch off and unplug

Switch off at the wall and unplug the machine, as long as the plug and socket are dry and you don't need to stand in water to reach them. If they're wet, switch the machine's circuit off at the DB board first.

### 3. Close the tap and disconnect the inlet hose

Turn the tap off fully. Put a bucket under the connection and unscrew the inlet hose from the tap, then from the back of the machine. A little water will run out of the hose.

### 4. Drain the water left inside

There's always some water left in the pump and the bottom of the tub.

- **Front loaders:** lay towels down and put a shallow tray under the pump filter flap at the bottom front. If there's a small emergency drain hose beside the filter, unclip it, take out its cap and let the water run into the tray, emptying the tray as often as you need to. Then refit the cap.
- **Top loaders:** unhook the drain hose from the standpipe or sink and lower its end into a bucket or a floor drain, so the water left in the machine can run out.

### 5. Disconnect and secure the drain hose

Unhook the drain hose from the standpipe or loosen it from the sink trap. Tape the hoses and the power cable to the back of the machine, or put the loose inlet hose in the drum, so nothing drags or snags on the way.

### 6. Refit the transit bolts (front loaders)

Take out the plastic caps that cover the bolt holes on the back panel. Screw each bolt in, with any sleeves or spacers your manual mentions, and tighten them until they're firm. Once they're in, gently push the drum through the door opening: it should hardly move. If it still swings freely, a bolt hasn't engaged properly.

### 7. Close everything up

Tape the door shut on a front loader, or the lid on a top loader, and tape the detergent drawer closed so it can't slide out.

## Moving it without damage or injury

Washing machines are heavier than they look, mostly because of the concrete counterweights that keep the drum steady, so this is always a two-person job.

- **Lift from the base,** with a firm grip under the bottom edges, bending your knees and keeping your back straight. Never lift by the door, the detergent drawer or the top panel, which can come loose.
- **Use a hand trolley with straps.** Tilt the machine just enough to slide the trolley under it, strap it on, and wheel it rather than carry it.
- **Protect the floor.** Don't drag the machine across tiles, laminate or wooden floors on its feet, which can scratch the floor and bend the feet. Slide it onto a piece of hardboard or thick cardboard instead.
- **On stairs,** keep one person below the machine and take one step at a time.
- **In the vehicle,** stand it upright, as Samsung advises, strap it to the side so it can't tip, and don't stack heavy boxes on top of it.

If the machine stands on a separate plinth or pedestal, move the two separately and check the manual for how the machine fixes to it.

## Setting it up at the new home

### 1. Position it

Put the machine within reach of the tap, the drain and a proper wall socket. AEG warns against running a washing machine from an extension lead, so if the socket is too far away, have an electrician add one. A solid, level floor is best, because a springy floor makes the machine vibrate more on the spin.

### 2. Take the transit bolts out

Before anything else, remove all the transit bolts, along with any sleeves, and fit the plastic caps into the holes so nothing can get in. Bosch recommends keeping the bolts and sleeves in case you move the machine again, so bag them and tape them somewhere you'll find them.

### 3. Connect the inlet hose

Check that the rubber washer is in place inside each end of the hose, then screw it onto the tap and the machine. Hand tight is usually enough; overtightening can crack the plastic fitting.

### 4. Connect the drain hose

Hook the drain hose into the standpipe or connect it to the sink trap at the height your manual gives. Secure it so it can't jump out when the pump pushes water through, and make sure the end of the hose doesn't sit below the water line in the standpipe or sink. Bosch warns that a hose end sitting in drained water can let dirty water be drawn back into the machine.

### 5. Level the machine

Put a spirit level on top, front to back and side to side, and adjust the feet until it's level. Bosch's instructions say all four feet must stand firmly on the floor, with the lock nuts tightened once the height is right. Press down on each corner in turn: if the machine rocks, adjust again.

### 6. Test it

Open the tap slowly and check both ends of the inlet hose for drips. Plug in, then run an empty rinse and spin or a short programme, as Beko suggests, and stay nearby. Watch for water under the machine, check that it drains, and listen for knocking or banging on the spin.

## Signs something went wrong in the move

| What you notice | Likely reason | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Violent shaking and loud banging on the spin | Transit bolts still fitted, or the machine isn't level | Stop and switch off; remove the bolts, then level the machine |
| The machine walks across the floor | One foot isn't touching the floor, or the floor is uneven | Re-level and tighten the lock nuts |
| Water at the back while filling | A loose inlet hose or a missing rubber washer | Close the tap, refit the washer and reconnect |
| Water stays in the drum | A kinked drain hose or a new sink connection that was never opened | See our guide to a [washing machine not draining](/problems/washing-machine-not-draining/) |
| Knocking or grinding that wasn't there before | Possible damage to the suspension or drum in transit | Stop using it and book a technician |

If the machine is noisy even with the bolts out and the feet level, our guide to a [washing machine noisy when spinning](/problems/washing-machine-noisy-when-spinning/) covers the other causes.
