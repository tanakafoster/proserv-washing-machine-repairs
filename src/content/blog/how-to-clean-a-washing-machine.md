If you want to know how to clean a washing machine properly, the answer is simpler than most cleaning hacks suggest: clear out the parts that trap dirt, run the machine's own cleaning programme with the product its manual approves, and then let it dry. That covers the detergent drawer, the door seal, the pump filter, the inlet filters and the drum itself, and it takes about an hour once a month.

The steps differ between front loaders and top loaders, and manufacturers don't agree on everything, vinegar especially. Below are the steps for each type of machine, what Samsung, LG and Bosch actually say in their own guides, and the signs that a machine needs more than a clean. If your main worry is a bad smell, our article on [why a washing machine smells](/blog/why-does-my-washing-machine-smell/) goes deeper into that, and if it's marks on your clothes, see our guide to a [washing machine leaving stains on clothes](/blog/washing-machine-leaving-stains-on-clothes/).

## What you'll need

You don't need special tools. Gather these before you start:

- **The manual for your machine.** It names the cleaning programme, the products it accepts and where the filters are. If you've lost it, see the questions at the end of this article.
- **An old toothbrush and two or three microfibre cloths.**
- **A shallow tray or roasting pan and old towels,** because water comes out of the pump filter.
- **A bucket,** if you'll be cleaning the inlet filters.
- **Rubber gloves.**
- **The cleaner your manual recommends,** or none at all if your machine's drum clean is meant to run empty.

<div class="callout callout-safety">

**Safety first:** switch the machine off at the wall and unplug it before you clean anything other than the outside, as long as the plug and socket are dry and you don't need to stand in water to reach them. If they're wet, switch the machine's circuit off at the DB board instead. Close the tap before you touch the inlet hose. Never mix cleaning products: bleach gives off toxic gas when it meets vinegar, acidic descalers or ammonia-based cleaners.

## How to clean a front loader, step by step

Front loaders hold water in more hidden places than top loaders, so they take a little longer. Work from the top down.

### 1. Empty the drum and check it

Take out everything, then run your hand around the drum and inside the folds of the door seal. Coins, hair clips, tissues and stray socks collect there, and anything left behind can scratch the drum or end up in the pump.

### 2. Clean the detergent drawer

Pull the drawer out until it stops. Most have a release tab, often in the softener section, that lets the drawer come right out. Many drawers also have a small removable cap or insert in the softener compartment, which lifts off for cleaning. Soak the parts in warm water, scrub the corners with the toothbrush and rinse them.

Don't forget the opening the drawer slides into. Shine a torch inside and wipe the walls and the roof, where water sprays in. LG's own guide points out that small bits of debris there stop the drawer from flushing properly, which makes compartments overfill and leak. Dry everything and slide the drawer back.

### 3. Clean the door seal

Fold back the rubber seal and work your way around it with a damp cloth, paying most attention to the bottom, at about six o'clock, where water and grit settle. What you clean it with depends on the manufacturer, and this is one place where their advice really differs:

- **LG** suggests about 177 ml of household bleach in 3.8 litres of warm water, wiped on with a cloth, or vinegar instead of bleach if you prefer, but never the two together. It warns that neat bleach makes the rubber deteriorate.
- **Bosch South Africa** advises against using vinegar to descale its machines because it's too aggressive for the rubber parts, and recommends its own washing machine cleaner.

So check your manual first. If it says nothing, warm water with a little mild detergent is the safe choice. Avoid scouring pads and powders, which scratch the rubber and give mould somewhere to grip.

### 4. Clean the pump filter

The pump filter (some brands call it the debris filter) sits behind a small flap low on the front of the machine, usually on the right or left corner. It catches lint, hair, buttons and coins before they reach the drain pump, and there's always some water behind it.

1. Make sure the last wash was a while ago, so the water inside has cooled.
2. Lay towels down and put the shallow tray under the flap.
3. If there's a thin emergency drain hose beside the filter, unclip it, pull out its cap and let the water run into the tray. Close it off when the tray fills, empty it and carry on until no more water comes out.
4. Turn the filter slowly anticlockwise and pull it out. Expect a last splash.
5. Rinse the filter under the tap and brush off any slime. LG says to use a soft brush and warm water and not to put the filter in a dishwasher.
6. Wipe out the opening, then screw the filter back in until it stops turning. Refit the cap on the emergency hose and clip it back.

On your next wash, glance at the flap for drips. A filter that isn't screwed in straight is one of the most common causes of a small leak after cleaning.

### 5. Wipe the door glass

Detergent and limescale leave a film on the inside of the glass that rubs onto the seal every time the door closes. A damp microfibre cloth usually shifts it. Wipe the door hinge area and the catch while you're there.

### 6. Run the drum clean programme

With the drum empty, run the cleaning programme your machine has, with whatever its manual says to add:

- **Samsung** machines have Eco Drum Clean or Drum Clean+. Samsung says the programme heats the water to between 60°C and 70°C to remove bacteria and dirt from the drum and door gasket, and that you should add no cleaning agents. A reminder light blinks every 40 washes.
- **LG** recommends a Tub Clean every month with a washing machine tub cleaner, and if a musty smell has already set in, a Tub Clean once a week for three weeks in a row.
- **Bosch** recommends its own tested washing machine cleaner.

If your machine has no cleaning programme, Electrolux's advice for its own machines is a useful guide: run the hottest cotton programme, usually 90°C or 95°C, with nothing in the drum and a washing machine cleaner added, and let it finish without interrupting it.

### 7. Let it dry

Wipe the seal and the glass dry once the programme ends, then leave the door and the drawer open for a few hours so the inside can air out. If you have small children or pets, close the laundry door instead, because an open drum is an inviting place to climb into.

## How to clean a top loader, step by step

A top loader has no door seal and, on most models, no front pump filter, so dirt collects in different places: under the rim of the tub, on and around the agitator or pulsator, in the lint filter, and in the dispensers. Many top loaders also don't heat their own water, which matters when you choose a cleaning programme.

### 1. Clean the lint filter

Many top loaders have a lint filter inside the tub, often a small mesh pouch or a plastic cartridge clipped to the tub wall, though some have one in the agitator instead. Unclip it, turn it inside out or open it, rinse off the lint and clip it back. If your machine leaves fluff on dark clothes, a full lint filter is a likely reason.

### 2. Clean the dispensers

Top loaders spread their dispensers around: a softener cup on top of the agitator on some models, and detergent, softener or bleach compartments in the rim or under the lid on others. Lift out the removable parts, soak them in warm water and scrub off the sticky residue with the toothbrush.

### 3. Scrub under the rim and around the agitator

Lift the lid fully and look under the rim of the tub with a torch. A grey or black line of residue usually builds up there, along with fluff and stray hairs. Scrub it off, then wipe the underside of the lid, the hinge area and around the base of the agitator or pulsator, where small items hide.

### 4. Run the tub clean programme

Use the tub clean programme if your machine has one, with the product your manual names. LG's tub clean advice applies to its top loaders as well as its front loaders. If your top loader only fills from the cold tap and has no heater, a hot clean isn't possible, so the cleaning programme and the right cleaner do the work instead. Check the manual before you fill it with very hot water from the kettle or geyser, because some plastics and seals aren't made for it.

### 5. Leave the lid up

Leave the lid open until the tub is dry, as long as no small children or pets can get to it. Wipe away any drips on the outside of the cabinet so they don't mark the paint.

## The inlet filters most people forget

Where the water hose screws into the back of the machine, there's a small mesh filter that stops grit and scale getting into the inlet valve. Samsung lists it among the parts to clean, and LG notes that these filters clog over time, especially in hard water areas, and that the machine shows an error when water isn't coming in properly.

In parts of Gauteng there's a local reason to check them too. When the water comes back after an outage, sediment from older pipes can arrive with it. Johannesburg Water has told customers that brownish water when the supply returns comes from calcified mineral deposits in older pipes, and that running the taps for a while flushes it out. Run a cold tap until the water is clear before you start a wash, and if the machine fills more slowly afterwards, the inlet filters are worth a look.

1. Switch off and unplug the machine, then close the tap completely.
2. Put a bucket under the connection and unscrew the hose from the back of the machine. A little water will run out.
3. Gently pull the mesh filter out of the valve inlet. LG suggests needle-nose pliers, used carefully so you don't damage the inlet.
4. Rinse it until it's clean. LG says the filter can be soaked in white vinegar or a limescale remover; rinse it well afterwards.
5. Push it back in, screw the hose on hand tight, open the tap slowly and check for drips before you plug in again.

If the tap is stiff, the hose looks perished or the connection is corroded, leave it for a technician rather than forcing it. A cracked fitting floods a laundry quickly. ## Vinegar, bicarbonate of soda and bleach: what manufacturers say

Cleaning hacks online often suggest vinegar and bicarbonate of soda for everything. Here's what three of the biggest washing machine brands say in their own support pages:

| Manufacturer | Cleaning programme | Vinegar | Bleach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Samsung | Eco Drum Clean, run with no cleaning agents added | Not mentioned in its drum clean guide | Not added to the drum clean |
| LG | Tub Clean every month, with a tub cleaner | Can replace bleach on the door gasket, and can be used to soak the inlet filters | Diluted only, for the gasket; never neat, never with vinegar |
| Bosch (South Africa) | Bosch washing machine cleaner | Advises against it for descaling: too aggressive for rubber parts | Not covered in its vinegar advice |

None of the manufacturer guides we checked for this article recommends bicarbonate of soda. The safest rule is the boring one: use what your own manual says, and if it's silent, stick to the machine's cleaning programme with a product made for washing machines.

## How often to clean each part

| Part | How often | When to do it sooner |
|---|---|---|
| Door seal (front loaders) | Wipe it dry after the last wash of the day, and clean it properly once a month | You see mould spots or grey slime |
| Detergent drawer | Every few weeks | Residue builds up, or water is left in a compartment |
| Pump filter (front loaders) | As often as the manual says | You wash a lot of sand, pet hair or work clothes, or draining slows |
| Lint filter (top loaders) | As often as the manual says | Fluff appears on your clothes |
| Drum or tub clean | Once a month, or when Samsung's reminder light comes on | The machine smells musty, or you mostly wash cold |
| Inlet filters | When filling slows down | After a water outage brings grit or brown water |

Measuring the detergent rather than pouring it means less residue builds up between cleans. Our guide to [how much washing powder to use](/blog/how-much-washing-powder-to-use/) has the doses for front and top loaders.

## When cleaning won't fix it

A clean sorts out most residue, but some problems only look like dirt. Book a technician if you notice any of these:

- **Black flakes on your washing after two or three drum cleans.** Heavy build-up between the drum and the outer tub can't be reached from inside. A technician has to open the machine to clean it out, which is part of what a [washing machine service](/services/washing-machine-servicing/) is for.
- **Water still sitting in the drum** after you've cleaned the pump filter. The pump or the drain hose may be the problem; see our guide to a [washing machine not draining](/problems/washing-machine-not-draining/).
- **A filter that's stuck, cracked or won't seal.** A broken filter cap or housing needs a new part, which our [parts replacement service](/services/washing-machine-parts-replacement/) can supply and fit.
- **A leak that started after cleaning** and doesn't stop once you've reseated the filter and the drawer. Our guide to a [washing machine leaking](/problems/washing-machine-leaking/) covers the other causes.
- **Water coming out of the drawer, or detergent left behind in it,** even when the drawer is spotless. That points to low water pressure, a blocked inlet filter or a weak inlet valve.
- **A burning smell, or the earth leakage tripping** at any point. Switch the machine off at the wall if the plug and socket are dry, or at the DB board if they're wet, then leave it off and call a technician. Our article on a [washing machine tripping the electricity](/blog/washing-machine-tripping-electricity/) explains what a trip means.

## The bottom line

Cleaning a washing machine is mostly about the hidden places: the drawer housing, the bottom of the seal, the pump filter, the lint filter and the little inlet filters behind the machine. Clear those, run the cleaning programme the way your manual describes, and let the machine dry. Done once a month, it keeps your washing fresher and catches small problems, like a slow drain or a perished hose, before they become repairs.
