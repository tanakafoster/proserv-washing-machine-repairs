A washing machine tripping the electricity is one of the few faults you should never try to work around. When the earth leakage unit or a breaker on your DB board switches off as the machine runs, a safety device has detected something wrong, and in most cases the problem is inside the machine: electricity escaping to earth through a heating element, a motor, a damaged wire or water where it shouldn't be.

Here's what the earth leakage actually does, the parts that most often cause the trip, how the moment it trips points to the culprit, and what you can safely do while you wait for a technician.

<div class="callout callout-safety">

**If your machine trips the power:** leave it off. Unplug it if the plug and socket are dry and you don't need to stand in water to reach them; if they're wet, keep its circuit switched off at the DB board instead. Don't plug it back in or switch it on to test it, even if the switch on the DB board resets, and never ask anyone to bypass the earth leakage.

</div>

## What the earth leakage is telling you

Earth leakage protection is compulsory in South African homes, and for good reason. Electricity should flow out along the live wire and back along the neutral. The earth leakage unit compares the two, and if some of the current goes missing, because it's leaking to earth through damaged insulation, water or a person's body, it switches the supply off in a fraction of a second.

It doesn't take much. Engineers at CBi-electric, a South African maker of these devices, point out that currents over 30 milliamps can be fatal, which is why household units are designed to trip at 30 mA or less. That's a tiny fraction of what a washing machine draws, so the unit will catch a small leak long before you would notice anything wrong.

A circuit breaker is different. It protects the wiring of one circuit from too much current, so it trips on a short circuit or when a circuit is overloaded. Both can be triggered by a faulty washing machine, but a trip of the earth leakage almost always means current is escaping somewhere it shouldn't.

| What tripped | What it reacts to | What it usually means for a washing machine |
|---|---|---|
| Earth leakage unit | Current leaking to earth, even a very small amount | Damaged insulation, water on an electrical part or a chafed wire inside the machine |
| Circuit breaker | Too much current on one circuit | A short circuit in the machine, or too many heavy appliances on the same circuit |

## The most common causes

UK appliance repair site Whitegoods Help lists the usual suspects when a washing machine trips the electrics, and they're the parts a technician tests first.

### The heating element

The element is a metal tube that heats the water. If its insulation breaks down, it leaks current to earth once it's switched on and warming up. It's one of the most common causes of all, and it explains why some machines run happily on a cold wash but trip a few minutes into a hot one.

### The mains filter (suppressor)

Many machines have a small interference filter where the power cable enters the cabinet. It's often the first part to receive power when the machine is plugged in, so a faulty one tends to trip the supply the instant the plug goes in or the machine is switched on. Power surges, for example when the electricity comes back after an outage, are hard on this part. Our guide on how to [protect your washing machine from load shedding](/blog/protect-washing-machine-from-load-shedding/) explains why.

### The motor

When the insulation inside a motor fails, the trip usually comes as the drum starts to turn after filling, and it tends to happen on the spin as well.

### Water on an electrical part

A leaking door seal, a split hose, a failing pump seal or an overflowing detergent drawer can let water run onto wiring, the motor or the control board. This kind of trip can be immediate or come and go, depending on where the water reaches and when. If you've noticed water under the machine as well, read our guide to a [washing machine leaking](/problems/washing-machine-leaking/) too.

### Chafed or damaged wiring

Inside the cabinet, the wiring harness moves with the tub. Over years, a wire can rub through its insulation against the frame or the drum and short to earth, typically on the spin with a heavy load, when the tub moves most. Wiring damaged during a move or a past repair can do the same.

### The plug, cable or socket

A cracked plug, damaged cable or worn socket can let in moisture or make a poor connection. These are easy to overlook because they sit outside the machine, but a technician checks them first.

## What the timing tells you

The moment the trip happens is the single most useful clue you can give a technician. Whitegoods Help sets out the pattern like this:

| When it trips | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| As soon as it's plugged in or switched on | Mains filter (suppressor), the cable or the plug |
| After filling, as the drum starts to turn | Motor, which will usually trip on the spin as well |
| Several minutes into a hot wash | Heating element |
| Only on the spin, especially with a big load | A bare wire touching the metalwork as the tub moves |
| At random, with no clear pattern | Water reaching an electrical part, or an intermittent wiring fault |

You don't need to run the machine again to find out. Think back over the last time it tripped: what was it doing, how far into the programme was it, and was it a hot or a cold wash? That information alone saves time on the visit.

## When the problem isn't the washing machine

Now and then the machine is innocent. These patterns point elsewhere:

- **The earth leakage won't stay on even with the washing machine unplugged.** The fault is somewhere else in your home's wiring or in another appliance. That's a job for a registered electrician.
- **Several appliances together tip it over.** CBi-electric notes that unexpected trips can come from leakage adding up across appliances, poor wiring, contaminated terminals or overloaded circuits. An older electrical installation with several appliances on one circuit is more prone to this.
- **A breaker, not the earth leakage, trips only when the kettle, heater and washing machine run at once on the same circuit.** The circuit may be overloaded rather than the machine faulty. An electrician can tell you, and in the meantime the machine should stay off until someone has checked.
- **The machine runs through an adaptor, multi-plug or extension lead.** AEG warns never to connect a washing machine through an extension lead, and adaptors add connections that can work loose or let moisture in.

A word on plugs. Some imported machines arrive with a European-style plug. CBi-electric explains that earthed Schuko plugs and sockets, the European type with earth clips on the sides, aren't allowed in South Africa, and that earth leakage devices only work properly when the installation complies with SANS 10142-1. If your machine depends on an adaptor, ask an electrician to fit the correct socket. Don't cut the factory plug off yourself, because that can affect the warranty.

## What you can safely do while you wait

There's no safe way for an owner to test the electrics of a washing machine. A tripping fault often only shows up under full mains voltage, which is why technicians use an insulation tester rather than an ordinary multimeter. What you can do is keep everyone safe and gather the details:

1. **Keep the machine off and unplugged,** if the plug and socket are dry. If they're wet, or water is on the floor around them, leave the machine's circuit off at the DB board.
2. **Don't keep resetting the earth leakage** to try again. Every trip means current took a path it shouldn't, and repeated trips can damage other parts.
3. **Look, don't touch.** Check whether there's water under or behind the machine, but don't remove panels, pull wires or try to dry anything inside the cabinet.
4. **Write down what happened:** when it tripped, which device tripped, what else was running, and any code on the display.
5. **Find the model number** on the sticker around the door opening, on the back or under the lid of a top loader.
6. **If the door is stuck shut** with washing inside after the trip, don't force it. Our guide to a [washing machine door that won't open](/problems/washing-machine-door-wont-open/) explains why, and the technician can release it safely.

If the machine also seems completely dead once the power is restored to the rest of the house, our guide to a [washing machine that won't turn on](/problems/washing-machine-wont-turn-on/) covers the other things that can cut its power.

## How a technician finds the fault

A technician starts at the socket and works inwards. They check the plug and cable, then test the main components with an insulation tester, which applies a high test voltage to show how well each part's insulation holds up. Each suspect, from the mains filter to the heating element, motor and wiring, is tested on its own, because the part that trips the supply isn't always the obvious one.

That's also why guessing at parts is a false economy. Whitegoods Help gives a good example: unplugging the motor can stop the trips simply because doing so moves a chafed wire away from the metal it was touching, so a new motor gets fitted and the fault comes straight back. Proper testing finds the real cause first.

Once the faulty part is found, it's replaced, the machine is tested again with the insulation tester and then run through a programme to confirm the fault is gone. ## Preventing it from happening again

Not every electrical fault can be prevented, but a few habits lower the odds:

- **Deal with leaks straight away.** A small drip from a hose or seal is the most common way water reaches electrical parts.
- **Use the right detergent and dose.** Too much detergent, or hand-wash powder in an automatic machine, can produce enough foam to push water out through the drawer and around the seal.
- **Give the machine its own properly installed socket** close by, so there's no need for adaptors or extension leads.
- **Protect it from surges** after outages and in summer storms.
- **Keep it level and the transit bolts out.** A machine that bangs and walks across the floor shakes its wiring and connections loose over time.
- **Have it serviced now and then.** A [washing machine service](/services/washing-machine-servicing/) includes a check of hoses and seals, which catches leaks before they reach anything electrical.

## The bottom line

A washing machine that trips the electricity has a fault that needs testing, and the earth leakage unit has done exactly what it's there to do. Leave the machine off, don't keep resetting the supply, and note when and how it tripped. With that information, a technician with an insulation tester can usually find the faulty part on the first visit, and most of the parts involved are replaceable.
