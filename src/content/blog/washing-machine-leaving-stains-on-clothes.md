A washing machine leaving stains on clothes is one of the most frustrating laundry problems, because the washing comes out looking worse than it went in. The good news is that most marks have nothing to do with a faulty machine. Samsung's own support team says the vast majority of stains that appear after a wash aren't caused by the washer at all, but by detergent, softener, water, or something in the load.

The colour and feel of the mark are the best clues to what caused it. Below is a guide to each kind of mark, how to get it out, a simple test to tell whether the machine is to blame, and the few signs that do mean a repair.

## Match the mark to the cause

| What the mark looks like | Most likely cause | Is it the machine? |
|---|---|---|
| White or powdery streaks | Undissolved detergent, too much powder, overloading or hard water | Rarely; check the drawer flushes properly |
| Dull grey smudges | Detergent residue, a dirty machine, overloading or too little rinsing | Sometimes: a machine that needs cleaning |
| Greasy, translucent spots | Fabric softener used wrongly | No, unless the drawer is blocked |
| Small black or dark flecks | Mould and greasy residue from inside the machine | Yes: it needs a clean |
| Brown, orange or rust-coloured marks | Iron in the water, brown water after an outage, or rusty items in the load | Rarely |
| Brown greasy streaks, often with a rumbling noise | A failing seal letting grease reach the drum | Yes: book a technician |
| Coloured patches | Dye from another garment | No |
| Pale or white patches on coloured fabric | Bleach | No |

## White marks: detergent that didn't rinse out

White streaks and powdery patches are leftover detergent. The usual reasons are:

- **Too much powder.** The excess can't dissolve and rinse away, so some is left on the fabric. Our guide on [how much washing powder to use](/blog/how-much-washing-powder-to-use/) gives the doses for front and top loaders.
- **Hand-washing powder** in an automatic machine, which produces far more foam and residue than the machine can rinse away.
- **Powder poured onto the clothes** instead of into the drawer, especially on cool programmes.
- **An overloaded drum,** where water can't flow through the washing to rinse it.
- **A drawer that doesn't flush properly.** If you find powder left in the drawer after a wash, the water isn't sweeping it into the drum in one go. A clogged drawer, low water pressure or a partly blocked inlet filter are the usual reasons; see our guide to a [washing machine not filling with water](/problems/washing-machine-not-filling-with-water/).
- **Hard water.** Samsung explains that dull grey or white deposits can come from calcium in the water, or from a reaction between liquid softener, detergent and suds.

Samsung's tip for telling these apart: run a wash without any additives. If the residue still appears, the water supply is the source. If it only appears when you add softener or other products, the combination of products is the problem.

## Grey marks: residue and a machine that needs a clean

Grey smudges are often a mix of detergent residue and dirt that has settled back onto the fabric. Electrolux lists the main causes as detergent residue, a dirty machine, overloading and using detergent incorrectly. It also notes that frequent washes at 40°C or below, especially with enzyme-based detergents, let mould and greasy residue build up inside the drum, seals and pipes, which then transfers to clothes.

Its suggestions, in short:

1. Run a monthly maintenance wash on the hottest cotton programme with a washing machine cleaner and nothing in the drum.
2. Use the dose the detergent maker recommends, and don't overdose.
3. Leave room in the drum so clothes can move and rinse properly.
4. Use an extra rinse if residue remains, and check the water pressure if rinsing seems weak.
5. Wash heavily soiled items separately, and keep lint-shedding towels away from delicate fabrics.

Electrolux also warns that personal care products can react with detergent and leave grey marks. Household bleach, face cream, shampoo, perfume, hair gel and hair dye are on its list, so a garment that came into contact with any of them may be marked even when the machine is perfectly clean.

## Greasy spots: fabric softener

Translucent, greasy-looking spots that feel slightly waxy are typical fabric softener marks. Samsung says they happen when softener is poured straight onto the clothes, put in the wrong compartment of the drawer, filled past the MAX line, used in too large a dose, or held back by a dirty or blocked drawer.

Use the softener compartment (often marked with a flower), stay below the MAX line and measure the dose for the load. Clean the drawer and the small siphon cap in the softener compartment if your drawer has one, because a blocked siphon can let softener out at the wrong time. If marks continue, Samsung suggests trying a different softener.

## Black flecks: build-up inside the machine

Small black or dark brown flecks are usually pieces of the mould and greasy residue that build up in hidden parts of the machine: the folds of the door seal, the detergent drawer housing and the space between the drum and the outer tub. Samsung adds that spots of brown, grey, black or green mould can also form when clothes are left damp in the machine, so unload as soon as the programme ends.

The fix is a thorough clean. Our step-by-step guide to [cleaning a washing machine](/blog/how-to-clean-a-washing-machine/) covers the seal, drawer, filters and drum, and our article on [why a washing machine smells](/blog/why-does-my-washing-machine-smell/) explains how the build-up forms. Check pockets too: a pen, crayon, lip balm or chewing gum that went through the wash can leave dark marks that look like they came from the machine.

## Brown, orange or rust-coloured marks

Yellow, orange, red or brown marks are often rust. Samsung says they're usually caused by iron or manganese in the water supply, or by rusty pipes and geysers, rather than by the washing machine.

In Gauteng there's a common local trigger: water that comes back brown after an outage or maintenance. Johannesburg Water has explained that this brownish water comes from calcified mineral deposits in older pipes, and that running the taps for a while flushes it out. Before you start a wash after the supply returns, especially a load of whites, run a cold tap until the water is clear.

Other causes worth ruling out:

- **Rusty items in the load,** such as coins, hair clips, underwire or old zips. A rust mark that matches the shape of the item gives it away.
- **Hair and skin products.** Zanussi explains that some hair care products contain a fixing ingredient that bonds to fabric and turns into dark or brown spots after washing, which is why towels are so often affected.

## Brown greasy streaks with a noise: a warning sign

This is the one stain that does point to a fault. A drum or tub has seals that keep water away from the bearings and, on many top loaders, the gearbox. Repair guides on the US site Fix.com describe greasy marks on laundry as an early sign that those seals are failing, because water getting past a worn seal can carry grease into the tub and onto the washing. Once water reaches the bearings, it washes their grease away, and they start to wear.

If you're seeing brown greasy streaks along with a rumble or roar on the spin, more noise than usual, or water under the machine, stop using it and book a technician. Our article on the [signs of worn washing machine bearings](/blog/signs-washing-machine-bearings-are-worn/) explains what happens next, and our guide to a [washing machine noisy when spinning](/problems/washing-machine-noisy-when-spinning/) covers the other causes of noise.

## Coloured patches and bleach marks

**Dye transfer** shows up as patches of colour on lighter garments. Zanussi notes that darker fabrics can keep releasing small amounts of dye, and that the areas in contact with them absorb it, so the marks only appear after the wash. Wash new dark items separately for the first few washes and sort by colour.

**Bleach marks** are pale or white patches on coloured fabric. Samsung lists the causes as bleach poured straight onto the clothes, put in the wrong compartment, filled past the MAX line or overdosed. Unlike other marks, these are permanent, because the colour itself has been removed.

## Is it the machine? A simple test

If you can't tell whether the marks come from the machine, Samsung suggests a process of elimination:

1. **Check the water.** Fill a white bucket from a tap on the same supply and look for colour or sediment. If the water itself is discoloured or gritty, the problem is the supply, and a plumber or your municipality is the next call.
2. **Run an empty cycle with no detergent or additives.** If you see residue in the drum, run two more empty cycles to flush it out.
3. **Still seeing residue after the third empty cycle?** Then the machine needs a technician.
4. **No residue in the empty cycles,** but marks on your washing? The cause is something you add or something in the load: detergent, softener, other products or the clothes themselves.

## How to get the marks out

- **Act before drying.** Check washing as you unload it, and don't tumble dry or iron a marked garment until the mark has gone, because heat can set some stains.
- **Rewash residue and softener marks.** Rub a little liquid detergent into the mark first, then wash at the warmest temperature the care label allows. OMO recommends testing neat liquid on a hidden area, such as an inside seam, before pre-treating.
- **Leave rust marks for a rust remover made for fabrics,** and avoid chlorine bleach on them, because it can make rust discolouration darker.
- **Treat dye transfer straight away,** before it dries, with a product made for it. The longer it sits, the harder it is to shift.

## Habits that prevent most marks

- Measure detergent and softener, and put each in the right compartment.
- Use auto powder, never hand-washing powder, in a machine.
- Leave room in the drum.
- Empty pockets before every wash.
- Unload as soon as the programme ends.
- Clean the drawer, seal and filters regularly, and run a monthly maintenance wash.
- Run the cold tap clear after a water outage before you start a wash.

## When to call a technician

Book a visit if you see brown greasy streaks along with noise on the spin, residue that keeps coming after three empty cycles, rust on the drum itself, a torn door seal that snags clothes, or water left in the drum after the programme. ## The bottom line

Most stains after a wash come from detergent, softener, water or something in the pockets, and they stop once you change the habit behind them. Match the mark to the table above, try Samsung's empty cycle test if you're unsure, and keep the machine clean. Greasy brown streaks with a noisy spin are the exception, and they're worth having checked before the bearings fail.
