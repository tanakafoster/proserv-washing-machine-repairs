How much washing powder to use depends on three things: whether your machine is a front loader or a top loader, how dirty the load is, and what your pack says. For the auto powders most South African homes buy, that usually means about one 180 ml scoop in a front loader and one to one and a half scoops in a top loader for a normal load, and less for lightly soiled washing.

More isn't better. Too much powder doesn't make clothes cleaner; it leaves residue on them, makes the machine work harder to rinse, and is a common cause of leaks, smells and foam errors. Here are the doses on two popular packs, how to adjust them, where the powder should go, and why hand-washing powder should never go in a washing machine.

## The doses on popular South African packs

Detergent makers set the dose, and it can differ between brands, so the pack in your laundry is the final word. As a guide, here's what Unilever publishes for two of the best-known auto powders on South African shelves:

| Machine | OMO Auto washing powder | Sunlight Auto washing powder |
|---|---|---|
| Front loader | 1 scoop (180 ml) for heavily soiled washing; less for lightly soiled | 1 scoop (180 ml) for a normal load; 1½ scoops for bigger or heavily soiled loads |
| Top loader | 1½ scoops (270 ml) for heavily soiled washing; 1 scoop for lightly soiled | 1½ scoops (270 ml) for a normal load; 2 scoops (360 ml) for heavily soiled |

Two things stand out. First, top loaders get more powder than front loaders. Second, even two powders from the same company have different doses, which is why it pays to read the side of the box every time you switch brands.

**Quick rule:** level the scoop, use the light-soil dose for everyday washing and the heavy-soil dose only for genuinely dirty loads, such as work clothes, sports kit or muddy school uniforms.

## Why front loaders need less powder

A front loader lifts the washing and drops it through a shallow pool of water at the bottom of the drum. It uses far less water than a top loader, which fills its tub until the clothes are submerged. Less water means the same amount of powder is more concentrated, so a front loader needs a smaller dose to reach the same cleaning strength.

That's also why front loaders are more sensitive to overdosing. Extra powder turns into foam in that small amount of water, and foam cushions the clothes so they don't tumble properly, holds dirt in suspension and is hard to rinse away. The machine then has to work longer to clear it, if it can clear it at all.

## Hand-washing powder and auto powder are not the same

On supermarket shelves, many brands come in two versions: a hand-washing powder and an auto powder for automatic machines. They aren't interchangeable.

Hand-washing powder is designed to foam a lot, because foam helps lift dirt when you rub clothes by hand in a basin. In a washing machine, the constant tumbling or agitating whips that foam up far beyond what the machine can handle. Unilever, which makes OMO and Sunlight, states plainly that hand-washing powder shouldn't be used in a washing machine, in a top loader or a front loader. It warns that the foam can damage the machine, that residue builds up on clothes, and that the clothes don't come out properly clean because the formula isn't balanced for a full machine load.

The usual results are suds pouring out of the detergent drawer, water leaking around the door seal, machines that stop before the spin because the pump can't clear the foam, and washing that comes out with a white film on it. Look for the word "auto" on the pack, or "for automatic machines", before you buy.

## How to measure washing powder properly

Most overdosing isn't deliberate. It happens because powder is poured straight from the box, or the scoop is heaped. A few habits fix that:

- **Use the scoop that came in the box,** and keep it in the box so it doesn't go missing.
- **Level it off** against the edge of the box instead of piling it up.
- **Write your dose on the box** with a marker, for example "1 scoop, front loader", so everyone in the house uses the same amount.
- **If you've lost the scoop,** use a measuring cup: 180 ml is just under three-quarters of a standard 250 ml cup.
- **Don't add "a little extra for luck".** If clothes aren't coming out clean with the right dose, the problem is usually elsewhere, such as an overloaded drum, a programme that's too short or too cold, or a machine that needs a service.

## Where to put the powder

### Front loaders

Most front loaders have a drawer with three compartments: one for the main wash detergent, one for fabric softener and one for a pre-wash. On many machines the main wash compartment is marked "II" or "2", the pre-wash "I" or "1", and the softener compartment carries a flower symbol, but layouts vary, so check the labels on your drawer or the manual. Sunlight's advice for its auto powder is to put it in the main wash compartment, and not directly into the drum or any other section.

### Top loaders

If your top loader has a detergent dispenser, use it. If it doesn't, add the powder to the empty drum before the washing, as Sunlight advises, so it can start dissolving before the clothes go in.

### Liquids and capsules

OMO says its auto liquids can go either in the drawer or straight into the drum, on top of the washing or at the back of the drum. Capsules are different: one per load, placed at the back of the empty drum before the clothes, never in the drawer, and only on a programme warm enough for the capsule to dissolve.

## Adjusting the dose for your load

The doses above are for a normal load. Adjust from there:

- **Lightly soiled clothes,** like shirts worn once or clothes that just need freshening, take the lower dose or less. This is most everyday washing.
- **Heavily soiled loads** take the higher dose. Pre-treat the worst stains with a little liquid detergent rather than adding more powder to the whole load.
- **Small or half loads** need less powder, not the same amount.
- **Hard water** can call for a slightly higher dose. Electrolux advises using a detergent suited to your water hardness and adjusting the dose to match.
- **The packs we checked dose by machine type and how dirty the load is, not by drum size.** So a 7 kg front loader with a normal load takes the pack's normal front loader dose. The higher dose is for a big drum packed with a genuinely dirty load.

Don't overload the drum to save powder either. Electrolux points out that an overloaded drum can't rinse properly, which leaves detergent and dirt behind on the clothes as grey marks. As a rule of thumb, you should be able to fit your hand flat on top of the washing in a front loader.

## Signs the dose is wrong

| What you see | Too much powder | Too little powder |
|---|---|---|
| In the drum | Lots of foam, foam still there in the final rinse | Hardly any suds and greyish water |
| On the clothes | White streaks, stiff or scratchy towels, a soapy smell | Grey or dingy whites, grease left on collars and cuffs |
| In the machine | Slimy residue in the drawer and door seal, musty smells | Body odours not fully washed out |
| On the display | Extra rinses, a longer programme or a foam code | Nothing unusual |

If you recognise the first column, cut the dose down by a quarter for a few washes and see how the clothes come out. Then run a hot empty maintenance wash to clear the residue already built up inside the machine. ## When too much powder has already caused trouble

If the machine is mid-programme and full of foam, let it carry on. Many machines detect excess suds and remove them before continuing. Don't open the door, and don't add cold water through the drawer to "wash the foam away", because that can push suds out of the drawer onto the floor.

If suds or water start coming out of the machine, switch it off at the wall, as long as the plug, the socket and the floor around them are dry. If they're wet, switch the machine's circuit off at the DB board instead. Once the foam has settled, run a rinse with no detergent. Then look at the guides that match what happened next:

- **Water on the floor** after the foam has gone: our guide to a [washing machine leaking](/problems/washing-machine-leaking/).
- **Water left in the drum,** or the machine stops before the spin: our guide to a [washing machine not draining](/problems/washing-machine-not-draining/).
- **A code on the display** that doesn't clear: our guide to a [washing machine showing an error code](/problems/washing-machine-showing-error-code/).

A machine that has had hand-washing powder through it for months may have a thick build-up of residue in the pump, hoses and between the drum and the tub. A [washing machine service](/services/washing-machine-servicing/) clears that out and checks for damage to seals and hoses.

## The bottom line

Use an auto powder, level the scoop and follow the pack: roughly one scoop in a front loader and one to one and a half in a top loader for a normal load, with less for light soiling and small loads. Keep hand-washing powder for the basin. Getting the dose right saves money on powder, gives cleaner clothes and spares the machine a lot of avoidable wear.
